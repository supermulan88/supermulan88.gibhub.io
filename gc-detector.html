The detection core

Molecular identification for gas chromatography.

A detector that tells you what eluted, not only that something did — reading each compound's molecular fingerprint as it leaves the column. No vacuum system. No ion source. This is the technology beneath every AATG instrument.

The gap

Knowing that something eluted is not knowing what it is.

Gas chromatography separates a mixture reliably. Identifying what came out is the harder half, and most detectors cannot do it. Flame ionisation, photoionisation and electron capture report that a compound passed, and when. Identity is inferred from retention time alone.

Mass spectrometry identifies properly, and it brings vacuum hardware, a service contract, a trained operator and usually a core facility. That cost is why so much volatile analysis still runs on a retention time and a best guess.

Identification

By structure, not position

Each compound is identified from its own molecular fingerprint rather than from where it happens to land in the chromatogram.

Separation

Partial separation is enough

Because every peak carries a molecular fingerprint as well as a retention time, compounds that overlap in time can still be told apart. The separation does not have to be perfect.

Trust

A confidence on every call

Results carry a calibrated confidence, so a non-specialist knows when to rely on an assignment and when to send the sample out.

Status

Where the work stands.

The subsystems are built and running independently: fast gas chromatography column modules, the optical detection system on a sub-micron positioning stage, and the inference layer that turns an optical signature and a retention time into an identified compound.

Integrating them is the current work. The U.S. National Science Foundation has invited AATG to submit a full SBIR Phase I proposal under its Scientific Instrumentation topic.

Performance figures quoted elsewhere on this site are design targets for the intended product rather than measured results. We would rather say so.

Further out

Chemical-threat and explosive trace detection — the application area behind r-Guard and r-Trace — requires a different optical configuration from the one in use today. That is a later stage of the same platform rather than a current capability.
